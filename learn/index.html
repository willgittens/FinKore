01 / The recurring problem

Financial CRUD
isn't ordinary CRUD.

The simple part is storing a record. The harder part is defining what its value, state, and history actually mean.

01

An amount looks like a number.

Until currencies, precision, rounding, and allocation determine whether its value is preserved.

02

An approval looks like a status.

Until the facts that justified the decision change, and you need to know whether it still applies.

03

An audit trail looks like a list.

Until someone needs to establish what happened, who acted, and which original facts were preserved.

04

A workflow looks like a few states.

Until a rejected transition leaves a partial change, or a new definition changes the meaning of an old instance.

The familiar cycle

  1. Build
  2. Discover edge cases
  3. Fix
  4. Document
  5. Rebuild elsewhere

The rules end up scattered across code, tests, documentation, and history. The next team rediscovers them.

02 / The boundary

Your product may be unique.
Your financial rules
don't have to be.

Your business logic stays yours.

Your application owns product policy, thresholds, permissions, UX, APIs, storage, and architecture.

FinKore focuses on the recurring financial behavior underneath those decisions: what should happen, what should fail, and what must remain observable.

04 / FinKore Core domains

The recurring foundations.

Organized around financial behavior:
values, decisions, state, identity, and history.

01 / Value

Money ↗

Exact amounts and currencies. Explicit arithmetic, rounding, allocation, and portable serialization.

02 / State

Workflow ↗

Declared lifecycle states, permitted transitions, guards, and unchanged state after rejection.

03 / History

Audit ↗

Attributable, append-only logical history. Events, changes, evidence relationships, and declared redaction.

04 / Decisions

Approvals ↗

Required business decisions, their factual basis, current validity, and declared combinations.

05 / Identity

Financial Entities ↗

Stable identity over time. Lifecycle rules, permitted changes, validation, revisions, and evidence-preserving evolution.

FinKore Core 2.0.2 stable. Concrete invoice, expense, ledger, payment, and tax models remain outside the current Core scope.

06 / Inspectable by design

Show the logic.
Don't sell the magic.

Look for the requirement, the acceptance case, and the connection between them.

  • Explicit specifications and stable requirement IDs
  • Portable cases with observable expected outcomes
  • Traceability and documented coverage limitations

Acceptance cases provide bounded evidence. They do not establish exhaustive correctness, production readiness, or regulatory compliance.
